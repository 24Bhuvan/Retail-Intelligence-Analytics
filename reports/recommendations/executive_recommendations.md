# Executive Recommendations

## 1. Executive Summary

This report translates the validated business insights from Phase 19 into prioritized executive recommendations.

The recommendations focus on five areas where the analysis provides a sufficiently strong evidence base for business action or further investigation:

1. Customer retention and repeat-customer participation
2. Delivery performance and customer experience
3. One-star customer experiences
4. Category revenue concentration
5. Regional revenue concentration and expansion evaluation

The recommendations are intentionally evidence-based. They do not assume a causal relationship where the analysis established only an association, and they do not quantify future revenue, retention, rating, or operational improvements without supporting evidence.

The highest-priority opportunity is customer retention because only 2,997 of 96,096 customers were identified as repeat customers, producing a validated repeat-customer rate of 3.12%. Delivery performance and one-star customer experiences are also high-priority areas because both provide measurable signals related to customer experience.

Category and regional recommendations are classified as medium priority. They describe areas for management attention and further analysis rather than asserting that concentration is inherently negative.

## 2. Priority Recommendations

### Recommendation 1 — Improve Repeat-Customer Participation

**Priority: HIGH**

#### Supporting Insight

Only 2,997 of 96,096 customers were classified as repeat customers, producing a validated repeat-customer rate of **3.12%**. The observed customer population is therefore dominated by one-time purchasers.

This finding was validated in the final business-insight analysis and is not based on a causal assumption.

#### Business Problem / Opportunity

The current customer mix indicates limited repeat purchasing relative to the overall customer population. This makes customer retention an important area for management attention and separate analysis from acquisition-driven sales.

The analysis identifies the size of the repeat-customer segment, but it does not establish why customers do not return.

#### Recommendation

Develop and evaluate a structured customer-retention initiative focused on converting appropriate one-time purchasers into repeat customers.

Potential mechanisms to evaluate include:

- Post-purchase customer engagement
- Targeted follow-up after completed orders
- Repeat-purchase offers
- Customer segmentation for retention campaigns
- Measurement of repeat purchase behavior by category, customer segment, and purchase history

These mechanisms should be tested and measured rather than assumed to be effective.

#### Expected Business Impact

The primary expected impact is an opportunity to strengthen customer retention and increase repeat-customer participation.

The recommendation should be evaluated against measurable retention metrics such as:

- Repeat-customer rate
- Number of repeat customers
- Second-purchase conversion rate
- Time between purchases
- Repeat purchase frequency

**No specific revenue increase or retention improvement is guaranteed by this recommendation.**

#### Assumptions

- The business can conduct post-purchase engagement.
- Customer-level purchasing history can be used for retention analysis.
- Some one-time customers may be suitable targets for retention activity.
- The 3.12% rate is a descriptive measure of the observed dataset, not a benchmark against another marketplace.
- The dataset does not identify the reasons customers fail to return.

### Recommendation 2 — Investigate and Improve Delivery Performance

**Priority: HIGH**

#### Supporting Insight

Among classified deliveries:

- **89.1%** arrived early.
- **7.9%** were late.
- Approximately **3%** were unclassified.

The analysis also found that weaker delivery outcomes align with lower review scores. The final insight explicitly treats this as an **association, not a causal relationship**.

#### Business Problem / Opportunity

Although most classified deliveries performed well, the late-delivery segment represents a meaningful operational population.

Because delivery timing and review outcomes move together in the observed data, delivery performance should be investigated as an important customer-experience dimension.

#### Recommendation

Conduct a structured investigation of late deliveries and identify the operational stages associated with delay.

The investigation should examine:

- Seller-to-carrier handoff timing
- Order processing duration
- Estimated versus actual delivery timing
- Geographic patterns in late deliveries
- Seller-level delivery performance
- Categories or order characteristics associated with delays
- Relationship between delivery outcome and review score

After identifying the major delay patterns, operational improvement actions can be evaluated and monitored.

#### Expected Business Impact

The expected impact is improved visibility into delivery reliability and the potential reduction of negative customer experiences associated with poor delivery outcomes.

Recommended monitoring metrics include:

- Late-delivery rate
- Average delivery duration
- Average handoff duration
- Late deliveries by seller
- Late deliveries by region
- Review score by delivery outcome

**The analysis does not support a quantified reduction in late deliveries or a guaranteed improvement in review scores.**

#### Assumptions

- The business can access or improve the operational processes associated with delivery.
- Late deliveries can be decomposed into meaningful operational stages.
- Delivery performance can be monitored consistently after intervention.
- The observed association between delivery timing and reviews does not prove that delivery delays cause lower ratings.

### Recommendation 3 — Address One-Star Customer Experiences

**Priority: HIGH**

#### Supporting Insight

The overall average review score is **4.09/5**, and **57.78%** of reviews are five-star. However, **11.51%** of reviews are one-star.

The evidence therefore indicates generally positive satisfaction while also showing a material segment of severe negative customer experiences.

#### Business Problem / Opportunity

A high average rating can conceal concentrated dissatisfaction. One-star reviews represent a sufficiently large segment to justify structured investigation.

The current analysis identifies the size of this segment but does not establish the root causes of dissatisfaction.

#### Recommendation

Establish a structured process for analyzing one-star customer experiences and identifying recurring issues.

The analysis should segment one-star reviews by available operational dimensions such as:

- Delivery outcome
- Seller
- Product category
- Region
- Order characteristics
- Review timing

Where review text is available and appropriate, qualitative review analysis can be used to identify recurring themes.

The resulting issues should be prioritized according to frequency, severity, and operational ownership.

#### Expected Business Impact

The expected impact is better identification and prioritization of customer-experience problems and a reduction in unresolved negative experiences.

Recommended monitoring metrics include:

- One-star review rate
- Average review score
- One-star rate by category
- One-star rate by seller
- One-star rate by delivery outcome
- Recurring complaint themes

**No specific improvement in average rating or reduction in one-star reviews is guaranteed.**

#### Assumptions

- One-star reviews contain actionable information about customer experiences.
- The business can investigate the operational causes of recurring issues.
- Review scores alone are insufficient to establish root cause.
- Additional qualitative analysis may be required before corrective action is selected.

### Recommendation 4 — Manage Category Revenue Concentration

**Priority: MEDIUM**

#### Supporting Insight

Category revenue is highly concentrated:

- Top 3 categories: **25.76%**
- Top 5 categories: **39.74%**
- Top 10 categories: **62.36%**
- 18 of 72 valid translated categories: approximately **81.2%**

This establishes a strong concentration pattern at the category level.

#### Business Problem / Opportunity

A relatively small group of categories contributes a large proportion of revenue. This makes category-level performance management important.

However, concentration is not automatically a business problem. The analysis does not establish that high concentration creates financial risk or that lower-contributing categories should necessarily be expanded.

#### Recommendation

Prioritize performance management of high-revenue categories while evaluating the commercial potential of lower-contributing categories.

Management analysis should consider:

- Revenue contribution
- Sales trends
- Order volume
- Average order value
- Customer demand
- Regional performance
- Category growth or decline over time

High-revenue categories should receive appropriate performance monitoring, while lower-contributing categories should be evaluated before investment or expansion decisions are made.

#### Expected Business Impact

The expected impact is improved visibility into category portfolio performance and better-informed category planning.

This can help management distinguish between:

- Categories that require protection and performance monitoring
- Categories with potential for expansion
- Categories whose low contribution may be structurally expected

**No revenue diversification, category growth, or revenue increase is guaranteed.**

#### Assumptions

- Category-level performance is strategically relevant to management decisions.
- Revenue concentration alone is insufficient to determine whether a category portfolio is healthy.
- Additional commercial information would be required before making investment decisions.
- The analysis does not contain sufficient cost or margin data to evaluate category profitability.

### Recommendation 5 — Evaluate Regional Expansion Opportunities

**Priority: MEDIUM**

#### Supporting Insight

Regional revenue is concentrated in a small number of states.

Validated values include:

- São Paulo: **R$5,202,955.05**
- São Paulo share: **38.28%**
- Rio de Janeiro: **R$1,824,092.67**
- Minas Gerais: **R$1,585,308.03**
- Top three states: approximately **63.37%** of sales

The final regional analysis uses the reconciled regional baseline. A conflicting SQL regional output was explicitly excluded from the final evidence.

#### Business Problem / Opportunity

The marketplace has a strong geographic concentration of sales. This makes regional performance an important dimension for planning.

The concentration itself does not prove that lower-contributing regions are underperforming or that expansion into those regions would succeed.

#### Recommendation

Conduct deeper regional analysis before making expansion decisions.

The analysis should compare regions using:

- Revenue
- Order volume
- Customer count
- Repeat-customer participation
- Average order value
- Delivery performance
- Review scores
- Seller coverage

Regions with lower sales but favorable customer, delivery, or order characteristics can then be evaluated as potential growth opportunities.

#### Expected Business Impact

The expected impact is stronger regional decision-making and identification of regions that warrant additional commercial investigation.

**No regional revenue increase or successful expansion is guaranteed.**

#### Assumptions

- Regional differences can provide useful information for market-planning decisions.
- Lower sales regions may contain opportunities, but this must be tested.
- Regional performance differences may reflect factors that are not available in the dataset.
- Additional market, customer, competitive, and operational information would be required before committing investment.

## 3. Implementation Considerations

The recommendations should be implemented as measurable business initiatives rather than treated as guaranteed outcomes.

### 3.1 Recommended Execution Order

| Order | Recommendation | Priority | Initial Action |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Improve repeat-customer participation | High | Segment one-time customers and design retention tests |
| 2 | Investigate delivery performance | High | Identify drivers and patterns of late deliveries |
| 3 | Address one-star experiences | High | Analyze recurring characteristics of one-star reviews |
| 4 | Manage category concentration | Medium | Establish category performance monitoring |
| 5 | Evaluate regional expansion | Medium | Compare regional opportunity indicators |

### 3.2 Measurement

Each recommendation should have a baseline and a post-implementation measurement period.

The current project provides useful baselines, including:

- Repeat-customer rate: **3.12%**
- Late-delivery rate: **7.9%**
- One-star review rate: **11.51%**
- Average review score: **4.09/5**
- Top 10 category revenue share: **62.36%**
- Top 3 regional revenue share: **~63.37%**

Future measurements should be compared against these baselines using consistent definitions.

### 3.3 Test Before Scaling

Where an intervention is proposed, the project should not assume that the intervention will work.

A stronger business process is:

1. Establish baseline.
2. Define intervention.
3. Test on an appropriate segment.
4. Measure the selected KPI.
5. Compare against the baseline or an appropriate control group.
6. Decide whether to scale.

This is particularly important for customer-retention initiatives, where the current dataset establishes low repeat participation but does not establish which intervention would improve it.

### 3.4 Additional Analysis Required

Before operational execution, management may require information that is outside the current dataset, including:

- Customer communication history
- Marketing campaign exposure
- Customer acquisition source
- Profitability and margin
- Inventory availability
- Carrier performance
- Operational capacity
- Competitive conditions
- Regional market size
- Customer demographics

These should not be inferred from the current Olist analysis.

## 4. Risks and Assumptions

### 4.1 Analytical Risks

**No causal inference**

The analysis identifies associations but does not establish causality. In particular, the relationship between delivery outcomes and review scores should not be interpreted as proof that delivery delays cause lower ratings.

**Population differences**

Customer metrics can differ depending on whether the analysis uses the full-order population or the item-backed population. The dashboard headline KPI uses the full-order population.

**Partial time periods**

The dataset contains incomplete periods at the beginning and end of the observed timeline. Near-zero late-2018 values should not be interpreted as a business collapse.

**Orders without items**

775 of 99,441 orders do not have order-item records. These orders are excluded from item-level sales, category, product, and related item-backed calculations.

**Category translation gaps**

1,627 order items lack translated category information and are grouped as Unknown/Untranslated where applicable.

**Regional evidence**

One SQL regional output conflicted with the reconciled regional baseline. The conflicting output was not used as final regional evidence.

**Monthly order-count differences**

Monthly revenue reconciles, but some monthly order counts differ between analytical outputs because of population/grain definitions. The final trend interpretation therefore emphasizes reconciled revenue rather than disputed order counts.

**No profitability conclusions**

The dataset does not provide sufficient cost, margin, or profit information to make profitability claims.

**No inventory conclusions**

The dataset does not support conclusions about stockouts, inventory turnover, or inventory availability.

**Review root causes**

Review scores describe customer satisfaction but do not, by themselves, identify why a customer was dissatisfied.

### 4.2 Recommendation Assumptions

The recommendations assume that management has the operational ability to investigate and act on the identified areas. The dataset alone cannot establish whether a recommended intervention is financially feasible, operationally feasible, or commercially optimal.

Therefore, each recommendation should be treated as a **decision-support recommendation**, not a guaranteed business outcome.

## Recommendation Governance Statement

These recommendations are intentionally conservative.

The analysis supports statements such as:

> "This should be evaluated as a retention opportunity given the 3.12% repeat-customer rate."

It does **not** support statements such as:

> "This initiative will increase revenue by 15%."

unless future testing provides evidence for such a claim.

The appropriate next step is therefore to use the recommendations to define measurable business actions, establish baselines, test interventions, and evaluate outcomes using consistent metrics.
